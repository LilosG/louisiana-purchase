## Louisiana Purchase: The Standard for Authentic New Orleans Creole

North Park's Louisiana Purchase leads the local Creole conversation. The restaurant opens with a bar scene that mimics the energy of Bourbon Street without the performance—bartenders who know their cocktails, a crowd that's there to eat and drink, not just pose.

The menu centers on Chef Austin's New Orleans background. He sources andouille sausage that actually tastes like something, sources Gulf shrimp when available, and builds stocks from scratch rather than shortcuts. The Alligator Andouille Cheesecake isn't novelty—it's a savory-sweet dish that demonstrates classical French pastry technique applied to Louisiana ingredients. The gumbo base uses a proper dark roux, simmered long enough to develop complexity.

The patio overlooks North Park's commercial strip, and while it's not the French Quarter, the space works. Live music runs select nights. The bar is a legitimate spot for post-dinner drinks, not just a restaurant that happens to serve alcohol—the [New Orleans cocktail menu](/menu/cocktails) is worth a second round.

**What it does best:** Technique and ingredient sourcing. If you want to understand how Creole food actually tastes when prepared correctly, this is the reference point.

**Logistics:** Full bar, patio seating, reservations recommended on weekends.

## What Makes It Real Creole Cooking

A proper roux takes time. Louisiana Purchase's kitchen builds its gumbo the traditional way, layering flavor over hours rather than rushing it. That commitment carries through the rest of the menu, from the Uptown Seafood Gumbo to Catfish Etouffee served blackened or fried.

## The North Park Setting

The dining room and bar bring a New Orleans energy to a North Park block, with a cocktail program built around classic and modern Creole drinks. Whether it's a weeknight dinner or a weekend crowd, the space handles both comfortably.

## Why Louisiana Purchase Stands Out

Louisiana Purchase distinguishes itself through three factors: technique consistency, ingredient sourcing, and atmosphere.

Louisiana Purchase treats Creole cooking as a foundation, not a cuisine to sample. Chef Austin's background means the kitchen understands why Creole techniques work—why you sear before braising, why you build stock from bones, why roux color matters.

Ingredient sourcing matters. Real andouille tastes different from generic sausage. Gulf shrimp tastes different from farm-raised substitutes.

Atmosphere isn't decoration. The bar at Louisiana Purchase functions as a real bar—people come for cocktails and stay for dinner, or vice versa.
